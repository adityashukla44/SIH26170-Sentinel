The burn-in screen

One run. Three verdicts.

Healthy parts stay with their batch. A latent defect looks normal at 24 hours and then climbs, so its forecast stays low and the measured 168-hour current is what catches it. An obvious failure drifts from the start, and the forecast sees it coming.

How screening works

From CSV to verdict in one upload.

  1. 01

    Upload the burn-in CSV

    One row per component with its 0, 24, 96 and 168 hour currents. The columns are checked in your browser before anything is sent.

  2. 02

    Score against the batch

    Five weighted signals compare each part with its batch's mean, median and spread, and with how far it drifted between stages.

  3. 03

    Forecast and decide

    The drift model forecasts the 168 hour current from the first 24 hours. Every part gets PASS, REVIEW or FAIL with the reasons that fired.

Three verdicts

Every part leaves with a decision.

Pass

Score under 35 · low risk

Behaves like the rest of its batch. Cleared for flight.

Review

Score 35 to 70 · medium risk

Unusual enough that an engineer signs off before it flies.

Fail

Score 70 or more · high risk

Too far from its batch to trust in flight. Rejected from the lot.

Method

How a component earns its verdict

Each component is judged against the batch it was burned in with, not against a fixed limit. The same rules run in the API and in every view here.

Pipeline

  1. 01CSV uploadOne row per component, currents in µA.
  2. 02Dataset validationRequired columns, numeric values, no gaps, unique IDs.
  3. 03Batch statisticsMean, median and standard deviation of the 168 h current.
  4. 04Anomaly signalsFive signals, each scaled 0 to 1, then weighted.
  5. 05Drift analysisStage drift 0→24, 24→96, 96→168 h and total drift.
  6. 06168 h forecastDrift model on the 0 h and 24 h readings.
  7. 07Risk scoringWeighted score, strong-evidence floors, forecast escalation.
  8. 08ExplanationPlain-language reasons for every flag that fired.
  9. 09DecisionPASS, REVIEW or FAIL with a risk level.

Anomaly score, 0 to 100

Each signal saturates at the limit shown, so one extreme reading can't swamp the rest.

SignalMeasured asSaturates atWeight
Deviation from batch mean|168 h − mean| ÷ mean100%30%
Deviation from batch median|168 h − median| ÷ median150%20%
Statistical outlier|z-score| at 168 h3σ15%
Total burn-in drift|168 h − 0 h| ÷ 0 h100%20%
Forecast growth|forecast − 24 h| ÷ 24 h100%15%

Strong-evidence floors

  • 168 h current 100% or more from the batch mean score ≥ 80
  • 168 h current 200% or more from the batch mean score ≥ 90
  • Current at least doubled during burn-in score ≥ 75
  • Forecast at least double the 24 h reading score ≥ 75

Decision

  • FailScore 70 or more · high risk · reject from lot
  • ReviewScore 35 to 70 · medium risk · engineer sign-off
  • PassScore under 35 · low risk · cleared

Forecast escalation

  • Forecast growth of 100% or more after 24 h → FAIL
  • Forecast growth of 50% or more on a PASS → REVIEW

The 168 h forecast

The drift model reads only the 0 h and 24 h currents. It learns how much a component grows between 24 h and 168 h from how far it drifted in the first 24 h: 168 h = 24 h × (a + b × early drift), fitted with robust Huber regression so the defective parts in a training batch can't drag it around. Working in growth ratios rather than raw microamps lets the model carry over to batches that run at a different current.

A screening line could stop burn-in early if the forecast is trustworthy, so the overview always checks it against the measured 168 h value and against the error measured in cross-validation when the model was trained.

The forecast falls back to linear extrapolation, 0 h + (24 h − 0 h) ÷ 24 × 168, when a component's early drift is outside the range the model was trained on, or when the model's answer isn't plausible. Every component reports where its forecast came from: ML_MODEL, FALLBACK_OUT_OF_RANGE, FALLBACK_UNSAFE_MODEL, FALLBACK_MODEL_ERROR or FALLBACK_NO_MODEL.
